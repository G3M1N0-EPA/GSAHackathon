DO NOT CALL VIOLATION — ACTION STEPS
======================================

The following steps outline the formal actions you can take against {caller_number} for
Do Not Call Registry violations. Estimated potential recovery: {total_damages}.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1 — FILE A COMPLAINT WITH THE FTC
----------------------------------------------------------------------

URL: {ftc_complaint_url}

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) enforces the National Do Not Call Registry and the
Telemarketing Sales Rule (TSR), 16 C.F.R. Part 310. Filing a complaint is free, takes
approximately 5–10 minutes, and strengthens the FTC's enforcement record against repeat
violators. Fines levied on companies can reach $50,120 per violation.

Instructions:
  a. Visit {ftc_complaint_url} in your web browser.
  b. Select "Unwanted Calls, Emails, and Texts" as the category.
  c. Choose "Unwanted telemarketing, robocalls, or text messages."
  d. Enter the caller's number: {caller_number}
  e. Provide the date(s) of the call(s) and a brief description of what was said.
  f. Submit the complaint. You will receive a confirmation number — keep it for your records.
  g. Note: The FTC does not resolve individual complaints but uses the data to build cases
     against high-volume violators.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2 — FILE A COMPLAINT WITH THE FCC
----------------------------------------------------------------------

URL: {fcc_complaint_url}

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) enforces the Telephone Consumer Protection Act
(TCPA), 47 U.S.C. § 227, and FCC regulations at 47 C.F.R. § 64.1200. An FCC complaint is
separate from the FTC complaint and targets the specific TCPA violation.

Instructions:
  a. Visit {fcc_complaint_url} in your web browser.
  b. Click "Phone" as the complaint category.
  c. Select "Unwanted Calls" and then "Telemarketing (including do-not-call violations)."
  d. Enter the caller's number: {caller_number}
  e. Describe each call with date, time, and what the caller said.
  f. Attach any records you have (call logs, voicemails, prior written notices you sent).
  g. Submit the complaint. The FCC may forward it to the caller and can open an investigation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3 — PURSUE CIVIL ACTION IN SMALL CLAIMS COURT ({state})
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Small Claims Limit for {state}: {small_claims_limit}

Under 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5), you have a private right of action to sue the caller in state
court for each violation of the Do Not Call Registry rules. You do NOT need an attorney to
file in small claims court.

Potential recovery:
  - $500 per call in statutory damages (minimum)
  - Up to $1,500 per call if the court finds the violation was knowing or willful
  - Total potential recovery for documented violations: {total_damages}

Instructions:
  a. Gather your evidence:
       - Call logs from your phone showing {caller_number} and the date/time of each call
       - Screenshot or confirmation of your Do Not Call Registry registration
       - Copies of any cease-and-desist letters you sent and proof of delivery
       - Voicemail recordings if available
  b. Identify the caller's legal name and address:
       - Search your state's Secretary of State business registry for the caller's company
       - Use the FCC's Robocall Mitigation Database: https://fccprod.servicenowservices.com/rmd
       - A reverse phone lookup service may also help identify the carrier
  c. File your claim in {state} small claims court:
       - Find your local courthouse at: https://www.uscourts.gov/court-locator
       - Complete the small claims petition form (usually available at the courthouse or
         the court's website)
       - Filing fees are typically $30–$100 depending on the county
       - Serve the defendant according to your state's rules (usually certified mail or
         process server)
  d. At your hearing, present your evidence and cite:
       - 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5) — TCPA private right of action
       - 47 U.S.C. § 227(c)(5)(C) — treble damages for willful violations
       - Your registration on the National Do Not Call Registry
  e. If the claim exceeds the {state} small claims limit of {small_claims_limit}, consider
     filing in a higher court or consulting a TCPA plaintiff's attorney (many work on
     contingency for TCPA cases).

Total documented violations:       see violation record
